# Why AI? A 30-Minute Quaker Activity

## Overview

A focused 30-minute activity designed to help Quakers explore the deeper questions about AI: why it matters, why we should engage with it, and how it connects to our values and concerns. This activity assumes participants have a basic understanding of what AI is (from prior learning).

## Activity Structure

### **Opening Worship** (5 minutes)
- Centering silence
- Invitation to consider: "What brings us to this conversation about AI today?"

---

### **Why AI Matters: Connecting to Our Concerns** (15 minutes)

#### **Part A: AI and Our Testimonies** (8 minutes)
Facilitator-led discussion with brief examples:

- **Simplicity**: AI can simplify tasks, but does it simplify our lives or complicate our relationships with technology?
- **Peace**: How might AI be used for peacebuilding? What are the risks?
- **Equality**: Who has access to AI benefits? Who is excluded?
- **Integrity**: What does honesty mean when AI can generate content?
- **Stewardship**: What is our responsibility to the Earth when AI requires significant resources?

**Facilitator prompt**: "Which of these testimonies speaks most strongly to your concerns about AI?"

#### **Part B: Why Engage?** (7 minutes)
Small group reflection (3-4 people per group):

**Questions for discussion:**
1. Why is it important for Quakers to engage with AI rather than ignore it?
2. What opportunities does AI present for advancing our values?
3. What risks concern you most?

**Report back** (2 minutes): One person from each group shares one key insight.

---

### **Quaker Queries for Reflection** (8 minutes)

**Individual reflection** (3 minutes of silence):
- How does AI relate to our testimony to truth?
- What does it mean to seek "that of God" in an AI world?
- How can we approach AI with both openness and discernment?

**Group sharing** (5 minutes):
- Invite participants to share insights from their reflection (no cross-talk, just listening)
- Focus on "What is emerging among us?"

---

### **Closing Worship** (2 minutes)
- A few minutes of silence
- Invitation to carry forward: "How will we continue this conversation?"

---

## Facilitation Notes

### **Before the Activity**
- Prepare a simple handout or slides with the testimonies and key questions
- Set up space for small groups (tables or chairs in circles)
- Have a timer visible to all participants

### **During the Activity**
- **Keep time strictly** - 30 minutes goes quickly
- **Encourage brevity** in sharing - remind participants we're gathering insights, not having full discussions
- **Honor silence** - don't rush the worship periods
- **Create safe space** - all perspectives welcome, no technical expertise required

### **After the Activity**
- Provide a resource list (handout or email) with:
  - Further reading on AI and Quaker values
  - Links to Quaker organizations working on technology ethics
  - Simple AI tools participants might explore
- Invite continued conversation (email list, next meeting, etc.)

---

## Adaptations

### **For Online Meetings**
- Use breakout rooms for small groups
- Share screen for queries and testimonies
- Use chat for brief reflections
- Build in intentional pauses for silence

### **For Larger Groups (20+)**
- Increase small group time slightly
- Have multiple facilitators
- Use a "talking stick" or similar method for large group sharing

### **For Groups with More Technical Knowledge**
- Add a question: "How can we help others in our meeting understand AI?"
- Include discussion of specific technical concerns (bias in algorithms, data privacy, etc.)

---

## Key Principles

1. **Start from where we are** - No one needs to be an AI expert
2. **Connect to Quaker values** - Ground the discussion in testimonies and queries
3. **Honor both concerns and opportunities** - Don't dismiss fears, don't ignore potential
4. **Keep it practical** - Focus on real-world implications and personal reflection
5. **Create space for continued learning** - This is a beginning, not an endpoint

## Why Would Quakers Want to Use AI?

This question often arises in discussions. Here are some ways AI can serve Quaker values and concerns:

### **Service to Others (Testimony of Love/Community)**

- **Accessibility**: AI tools can help make information and services more accessible to people with disabilities (text-to-speech, image description, language translation)
- **Social justice work**: AI can help identify patterns of inequality, analyze policy impacts, or support advocacy efforts at scale
- **Education**: AI can provide personalized learning support, especially valuable in underserved communities
- **Crisis response**: AI can help coordinate relief efforts, match resources with needs, or analyze disaster patterns

### **Peace (Testimony of Peace)**

- **Conflict resolution**: AI can analyze patterns in conflict, help identify early warning signs, or support peacebuilding efforts
- **Diplomatic support**: Translation tools and cultural understanding aids can facilitate cross-cultural communication
- **Non-violent alternatives**: AI can support non-violent solutions to problems traditionally requiring force

### **Equality (Testimony of Equality)**

- **Reducing barriers**: AI can help overcome language barriers, provide access to information regardless of location, or support equal opportunity in hiring
- **Amplifying marginalized voices**: AI tools can help translate, transcribe, or make visible voices that might otherwise be overlooked
- **Fair resource distribution**: AI can help identify and address systemic inequalities in resource allocation

### **Simplicity (Testimony of Simplicity)**

- **Efficiency in service**: AI can handle routine administrative tasks, freeing time for direct service and relationship-building
- **Meeting administration**: AI could help with scheduling, record-keeping, or accessibility accommodations, allowing more time for worship and discernment
- **Reducing duplication**: AI can help coordinate efforts across meetings and organizations, avoiding redundant work

### **Stewardship (Testimony of Stewardship)**

- **Environmental monitoring**: AI can help track environmental changes, optimize resource use, or identify conservation opportunities
- **Climate solutions**: AI can support research into renewable energy, carbon reduction strategies, or sustainable agriculture
- **Resource efficiency**: When used thoughtfully, AI can help optimize systems to reduce waste and environmental impact

### **Integrity (Testimony of Integrity)**

- **Truth-seeking**: AI can help fact-check, analyze data, or identify misinformation—though we must also be aware of AI's potential to generate false information
- **Transparency**: AI tools can help make complex systems more understandable and accessible
- **Accountability**: AI can help track and analyze impacts of policies or actions, supporting accountability

### **Important Caveats**

While these possibilities exist, Quakers must also consider:

- **Who benefits?** Not all AI applications serve equality; many reinforce existing power structures
- **What are the costs?** AI development requires significant resources, data, and energy
- **Who is harmed?** AI can perpetuate bias, invade privacy, or displace workers
- **What values are embedded?** AI systems reflect the values of their creators; we must discern whether those align with our own

### **The Quaker Approach**

Quakers don't adopt technology uncritically. We engage with AI:
- **With discernment** - Not all uses are appropriate
- **With awareness** - Understanding impacts and implications
- **With values** - Grounding decisions in our testimonies
- **With community** - Making choices together, not alone
- **With humility** - Acknowledging we don't have all the answers

The question isn't whether to use AI, but **how** to use it—and whether to use it at all in specific contexts—in ways that align with our values and serve the greater good.

## Common Questions & Responses

**"I don't know anything about AI. Will I be lost?"**
- This activity is designed for complete beginners. We'll start with everyday examples everyone can relate to. Your questions and reflections are valuable regardless of technical knowledge.

**"Isn't AI just a tool? Why do we need to reflect on it?"**
- AI is a tool, yes, but it's a tool that makes decisions, influences behavior, and affects our relationships. Quakers have always engaged with technology thoughtfully—consider our history with industrialization, warfare, and social change. This is part of that tradition.

**"I'm worried about AI. Is this going to be depressing?"**
- We'll explore both concerns and hopes. The goal isn't to create fear, but to engage honestly with what is, and to discern how we might respond with integrity and love.

**"Can we use AI in our meeting?"**
- That's a great question for discernment. This activity is an introduction; deeper questions about practical applications can be explored in follow-up meetings or discussions.

## Notes on Quaker Process

This activity follows Quaker practices:

- **Worship** - We begin and end in worship, seeking the Light
- **Queries** - We use queries to invite reflection, not debate
- **Silence** - We value silence as a space for the Light to speak
- **Shared Discernment** - We listen together to what emerges
- **Testimonies** - We ground our reflection in Quaker testimonies
- **No Hierarchy** - Facilitator holds space but doesn't teach; all voices are valued
