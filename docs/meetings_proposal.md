# Quakers and AI: Monthly Meeting Series Proposal

## Overview

A series of 5 monthly meetings (90 minutes each) designed to explore the intersection of Quaker values and artificial intelligence. The series progresses from basic AI concepts to deeper ethical engagement, welcoming participants with no prior AI experience while incorporating insights from those with technical backgrounds.

## Meeting Topics

### **Meeting 1: "What is AI? A Quaker Introduction"**
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Level:** Complete beginners

**Structure:**
- **Opening worship** (10 minutes)
- **What is AI?** - Simple, non-technical explanation (20 minutes)
- **AI in daily life** - Examples everyone can relate to (15 minutes)
- **Quaker queries for reflection** (20 minutes)
- **Small group sharing** - "Where do you encounter AI?" (15 minutes)
- **Closing worship** (10 minutes)

**Key Questions:**
- How does AI affect our daily lives?
- What does it mean to be human in an AI world?
- How can we approach AI with integrity?

**Resources:** Simple videos, real-world examples (smartphones, online shopping, GPS)

---

### **Meeting 2: "AI and Quaker Testimonies"**
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Level:** Building on Meeting 1

**Structure:**
- **Opening worship** (10 minutes)
- **Review of Quaker testimonies** (15 minutes)
- **AI through the lens of each testimony** (40 minutes)
  - Simplicity: Does AI simplify or complicate life?
  - Peace: How might AI promote or threaten peace?
  - Equality: Who benefits from AI? Who is left behind?
  - Integrity: What does honesty mean with AI?
  - Stewardship: Environmental impacts of AI
- **Group discernment** - "What concerns us most?" (15 minutes)
- **Closing worship** (10 minutes)

**Key Questions:**
- Which Quaker testimonies are most challenged by AI?
- How can we use AI to live our testimonies more fully?

---

### **Meeting 3: "AI in Our Communities"**
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Level:** Intermediate

**Structure:**
- **Opening worship** (10 minutes)
- **Local AI examples** - What's happening in our area? (20 minutes)
- **Case studies** - Real Quaker communities using AI (20 minutes)
- **Breakout groups** - Different scenarios (25 minutes)
  - Group A: AI in meeting administration
  - Group B: AI for social justice work
  - Group C: AI in Quaker education
- **Report back and discussion** (10 minutes)
- **Closing worship** (5 minutes)

**Key Questions:**
- How might our meeting use AI responsibly?
- What AI applications align with our values?
- What are our red lines?

---

### **Meeting 4: "The Ethics of AI: A Quaker Perspective"**
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced

**Structure:**
- **Opening worship** (10 minutes)
- **Ethical frameworks** - Different approaches to AI ethics (20 minutes)
- **Quaker discernment process** - How do we make ethical decisions? (15 minutes)
- **Case study analysis** - Real ethical dilemmas (30 minutes)
  - AI bias and discrimination
  - Privacy and surveillance
  - Job displacement
  - Military applications
- **Developing our own guidelines** (10 minutes)
- **Closing worship** (5 minutes)

**Key Questions:**
- How do we apply Quaker discernment to AI ethics?
- What principles should guide our AI decisions?
- How do we balance innovation with responsibility?

---

### **Meeting 5: "Action and Advocacy: Quakers Engaging with AI"**
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Level:** All levels, action-oriented

**Structure:**
- **Opening worship** (10 minutes)
- **Recap of key learnings** (10 minutes)
- **Action planning** - What can we do? (40 minutes)
  - Personal actions
  - Meeting-level initiatives
  - Broader advocacy
- **Resource sharing** - Tools and organizations (15 minutes)
- **Next steps** - Continuing the conversation (10 minutes)
- **Closing worship** (5 minutes)

**Key Questions:**
- How do we want to engage with AI going forward?
- What actions align with our values and capabilities?
- How do we continue learning and growing together?

---

## Format Considerations

### **Meeting Structure**
- **Hybrid meetings** - Allow both in-person and online participation
- **Resource packets** - Send materials before each meeting
- **Guest speakers** - Invite AI experts, ethicists, or other Quakers
- **Field trips** - Visit local AI companies or research centers

### **Between Meetings**
- **Reading groups** - Articles, books, or videos
- **Online discussions** - Email lists or forums
- **Experimentation** - Try AI tools and share experiences
- **Research projects** - Investigate specific AI applications

## Resources to Prepare

### **Educational Materials**
- **Simple explainer videos** about AI
- **Quaker writings** on technology and ethics
- **Case studies** of Quaker communities using AI
- **Glossary** of AI terms
- **Reading lists** for different levels

### **Suggested Readings**
- "Quakers in Brief" by David Murray-Rust (for historical context)
- Articles on AI ethics from Quaker perspectives
- "Technology and the Soul" by various Quaker authors
- Current news articles on AI developments

## Facilitation Guidelines

### **Ground Rules**
- Respectful listening and sharing
- No technical jargon without explanation
- All questions welcome
- Confidentiality within the group

### **Facilitation Tips**
- **Small groups** - Mix experienced and inexperienced participants
- **Visual aids** - Diagrams, charts, real examples
- **Time management** - Keep discussions focused and moving
- **Follow-up** - Send summaries and resources after each meeting
- **Inclusive language** - Avoid assumptions about technical knowledge

### **Role of AI-Experienced Participants**
- Serve as resource people, not teachers
- Help translate technical concepts into accessible language
- Share practical experiences with AI tools
- Support group discernment rather than dominating discussion

## Expected Outcomes

### **Learning Goals**
- Basic understanding of AI and its applications
- Ability to evaluate AI through Quaker testimonies
- Confidence in discussing AI ethics
- Practical tools for responsible AI engagement

### **Community Building**
- Strengthened connections within the meeting
- Shared understanding of technology and values
- Collective wisdom on AI engagement
- Ongoing support network for AI-related questions

### **Action Items**
- Personal commitments to responsible AI use
- Meeting-level policies or guidelines
- Advocacy opportunities in the broader community
- Continued learning and discussion
